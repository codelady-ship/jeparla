# Jeparla competitor research

Date: 2026-11-09. Method: web search and page fetches. Some sites block automated fetches (tap.az
category pages returned 404, Tanishq 403, Malabar redirect loop), so parts of those rows come from
secondary sources (app-store listings, reviews, news) and are marked as such. Nothing here is
copied for use: only ideas and patterns (clean-room rule in CLAUDE.md). Numbers quoted from
third-party pages are their claims, not verified facts.

## Summary: what the market looks like

- **Azerbaijan has no jewelry aggregator.** Jewelry is sold through general classifieds
  (Tap.az, Lalafo.az), Instagram/WhatsApp pages, single retailers (Italdizain) and offline
  bazaars. Gold-per-gram AZN figures come from foreign tracker sites with no date, no source and
  numbers that disagree with each other (one showed 24K about 302 AZN/g in January 2026, another about 222 AZN/g,
  with no date). A CBAR-sourced, dated price is a clear gap Jeparla can fill.
- **Classifieds already structure jewelry data** (Lalafo: metal colour, əyar, stone, carat band)
  and put a WhatsApp button next to every ad, so Jeparla's bar is higher: verified data, price
  transparency, store identity.
- **India sets the bar for price transparency**: line-item price breakup (metal value, making
  charges, stones, tax) and public hallmark verification (BIS Care HUID lookup). Nothing similar
  exists in AZ.
- **Global marketplaces win on trust signals that are computed, not claimed** (Etsy Star Seller
  from measured response/shipping data; 1stDibs vetting by specialists; Jewelers of America
  member code signed every year).
- **Seller tools competitors in the Instagram-first segment** are link-in-bio and WhatsApp-catalog
  tools (Taplink, Take App), not marketplaces. Jeparla's seller pitch must beat them: free,
  automatic metal-linked pricing, buyer traffic from search, verification.

## Per-product notes

### 1. Tap.az (AZ, classifieds)
- (a) Features: huge reach; jewelry sits inside a general classifieds category tree; "Premium
  elanlar" block on the home page; `/shops` directory of paid shops.
- (b) Trust: "Mağaza" label on listings from paid shops; no jewelry-specific verification
  (no hallmark, no fineness check).
- (c) Design: dense listing grid, price-first cards; not jewelry-aware.
- (d) Motion: none of note.
- (e) Sellers: paid shop subscription and paid bumps are the business model; this is where
  AZ jewelers already are, so #100 (share link, `?ref=tapaz`) is the right bridge.
- (f) Marketing: brand is a household name; jewelers repost Tap.az ads to Instagram.
- Note: category pages blocked automated fetch (404), so detail is from the home page only.

### 2. Lalafo.az (AZ, classifieds)
- (a) Structured jewelry attributes: metal colour (sarı/ağ qızıl), əyar (585/750/925), stone
  type, carat band (e.g. "1.51-2.00 Karat"); ring size and weight usually only in free text.
- (b) Trust: "PRO" seller badge, last-online date, **measured response rate** ("replies to 62%
  of messages, usually within a few days"), report button.
- (c) Design: classic classifieds card; attributes repeated in the title.
- (d) Motion: none.
- (e) Sellers: "Biznes üçün" paid posting page; other ads of the seller listed under the ad.
- (f) Marketing: share button with WhatsApp share link on every ad.
- Takeaway: buyers in AZ are already used to əyar/stone filters and a WhatsApp button; Jeparla
  must add what classifieds cannot: verified data, price logic, store identity.

### 3. Italdizain (AZ, multi-brand luxury retailer)
- (a) AZ/EN/RU switcher, catalog by type and by brand, favorites, separate boutiques pages.
- (b) Trust: founded-year, official brand representation, named legal operators in footer,
  news/events.
- (c) Design: image-led, brand logos, boutique photos; each boutique has its own page with
  hours, phone, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook.
- (d) Motion: not determinable from fetch.
- (e) n/a (single retailer).
- (f) Events and press used as content.
- Takeaway: per-store contact block with hours and several channels is the local norm; Jeparla
  already shows hours and maps. Gap: Italdizain does not show weight/fineness/price logic.

### 4. Instagram-first jewelers + Taplink (AZ/CIS)
- (a) Link-in-bio page holding catalog, WhatsApp link with prefilled message, payment info.
- (b) Trust: only the Instagram follower count and stories; no data verification.
- (c) Design: template pages, often low quality.
- (d) Motion: template animations.
- (e) Seller tools: set up "in under an hour" according to user reviews; paid tiers.
- (f) The whole marketing loop is Instagram/TikTok -> bio link -> WhatsApp.
- Takeaway: this is Jeparla's real competitor for seller acquisition. Jeparla must be as fast
  to set up (bulk add) and give something these tools cannot (search traffic, metal-linked
  price, verification).

### 5. Take App (global, WhatsApp store builder)
- (a) Product pages with metals/sizes/options, store link shared on WhatsApp, order review page,
  orders arrive in WhatsApp.
- (b) Trust: none beyond the seller.
- (c) Clean mobile-first template.
- (e) Free tier: unlimited products, 50 orders/month, no commission; analytics and SEO only on
  the paid tier (29 USD/month).
- (f) Positioned by industry landing pages ("jewelry and accessories").
- Takeaway: an industry-specific "for sellers" landing page with a clear free-vs-paid comparison
  is a standard acquisition pattern.

### 6. Gold tracker sites with AZN pages (goldpricez, livepriceofgold, goldpricedata)
- (a) Per-gram price by karat (24K..8K) in AZN, charts.
- (b) Trust: weak: figures from different sites disagree strongly and some snapshots are
  undated; source is spot price, not CBAR.
- (c) Ad-heavy SEO layouts.
- (f) They rank for "qızılın qramı" style queries today.
- Takeaway: Jeparla's `/qiymetler` page (#61) with the official CBAR source and date, plus a
  calculator by weight and əyar, can outrank them on trust and on AI answers.

### 7. AzerGold (AZ, state bullion)
- (b) Trust: state company, published buyback volumes, buyback at its sales centre.
- Takeaway: buyback/exchange is a strong trust promise in gold markets (also CaratLane,
  Tanishq). Jeparla cannot promise it, but stores can declare their own exchange/buyback terms as
  a structured store attribute.

### 8. Trendyol (TR, multi-vendor marketplace, kuyumcu sellers)
- (a) Ayar filter (14/22 ayar), gram weight in titles; separate categories for bullion and
  jewelry.
- (b) Seller ratings, marketplace buyer protection (payments on-platform).
- (c) Dense mass-market layout; ayar tag under the title on cards.
- (e) Kuyumcu sellers get national reach.
- Takeaway: weight and fineness on the card itself is expected; Jeparla already shows
  `{metal} {fineness} . {weight}` on cards.

### 9. Harem Altın (TR, live price app)
- (a) Live gram, 22 ayar, 14 ayar, coins (çeyrek, yarım, tam); **price alerts** and a
  **portfolio** feature.
- (b) Known brand of a gold trading house; outages reported in press.
- (f) The app is a daily habit: users open it every day for the price.
- Takeaway: daily price is a retention hook. A "price changed since you saved it" signal on
  favorites gives Jeparla a reason to return without needing push notifications.

### 10. Yandex Market / Yandex Maps price lists (RU)
- (a) Market: filters by metal and proba (585, 925). Maps: each store's organisation card holds
  an item price list with proba and insert (stone) per item.
- (b) Maps reviews and ratings per branch.
- (e) Stores maintain a mini-catalog inside the map card: a store-locator + catalog hybrid.
- Takeaway: "near me" store discovery combined with each store's catalog is a proven model;
  Jeparla can sort stores by distance without a heavy map library.

### 11. 585 Zolotoy app (RU, chain)
- App store rating 4.9 (160 ratings, per app store page). Chain apps focus on loyalty and
  in-store pickup. Low relevance to the multi-store model, but confirms the
  "proba in the brand name" positioning: fineness is the key buying fact in CIS.

### 12. Kaspi Obyavleniya / OLX.kz (KZ)
- Classifieds full of 585 gold, many used items. Kazakhstan recently tightened rules and fines
  selling without an assay mark (kapital.kz). Takeaway: hallmark (assay stamp) is a legal and
  trust anchor across the region; the same is true in AZ (state agency briefed jewelry sellers
  on liability for items without əyar damğası, AZERTAC report).

### 13. Dubai Gold & Jewellery Group daily rate (UAE)
- (a) An official rate for 24K/22K/21K/18K per gram, published at fixed times each day (sources disagree whether
  two or three times); every retailer and news site repeats it; shop price = rate + making
  charges.
- (b) Trust comes from one shared reference rate.
- Takeaway: exactly Jeparla's CBAR model. Copy the pattern (not text): show the reference rate,
  its publication date/time and the store's making charge separately.

### 14. Tbilisi Gold Market / goldshop.ge (GE)
- Offline: hundreds of traders in one hall at Tbilisi Central station; guides advise asking for
  a receipt and hallmark certificate. goldshop.ge is a small online buy/sell marketplace with no
  reviews yet.
- Takeaway: the "one hall, many traders" idea is what Jeparla digitises; buyer advice content
  ("what to ask the seller") is valued.

### 15. Tanishq / Malabar / CaratLane + BIS Care (IN)
- (a) **Price breakup** on every item: gold value (rate x weight), making charges, stone
  charges, tax. CaratLane publishes the formula and a resale value calculator. Malabar's app has
  live rate + store locator; Kalyan launched live video calling for UAE buyers.
- (b) **Hallmark verification**: every piece carries a 6-character HUID; any buyer can check
  purity, jeweller registration, hallmarking centre and date in the free BIS Care app; a "record
  not found" is a red flag. Buyback/exchange promises.
- (c) Price breakup shown as a compact table under the price.
- (e) Store locator with phone per store.
- (f) Heavy education content (making charges explainers) used for SEO.
- Takeaway: the single most transferable idea set: price breakup on PDP, hallmark photo/code on
  the product, calculator and explainers.

### 16. Etsy (global marketplace)
- (b) **Star Seller** badge computed monthly from measured data: message response within 24 h,
  tracked shipping, ratings, minimum orders; sellers see their own progress; not achieving it is
  not penalised.
- (e) Seller dashboard shows progress toward the badge, which nudges good behaviour.
- Takeaway: Jeparla's trust signals should be measured facts (catalog freshness, how long on
  Jeparla, share of metal-linked prices, report resolution), shown with how they are computed.
  Response time cannot be measured honestly because chat happens in WhatsApp, so it should not
  be shown.

### 17. 1stDibs (vetted-dealer marketplace)
- (b) Sellers are vetted by staff specialists (gemology, appraisal) before joining; "Recognized"
  and "Top Seller" labels; public **seller directory** by category (jewelry).
- (c) Seller profile pages with history and location.
- Takeaway: a public "how we vet" methodology page makes the Verified badge meaningful; also
  say what vetting does not mean (it is not a lab authentication of every piece).

### 18. Rare Carat (diamond aggregator)
- (a) Aggregates many sellers' diamonds into one search; "Deal Score"/fair-price estimate per
  stone; recommends comparing by **price per carat**; shareable report page per stone.
- (b) Trust is driven by analysis, though the model is not published.
- Takeaway: Jeparla can show an honest, transparent equivalent for gold: the item's price per
  gram versus today's metal value per gram (pure arithmetic, no "deal" judgement).

### 19. Brilliant Earth / James Allen / Blue Nile (online diamond retailers)
- (a) Interactive drag-to-rotate video per item, 360 HD video with zoom (James Allen), side-by-side
  compare (Blue Nile), virtual appointments (Zoom, 50 min), showroom booking.
- (b) Lab certificates (GIA, IGI, HRD, GCAL) linked per stone.
- (d) The video is the main motion element and it is user-driven, not autoplay.
- Takeaway: a short seller-shot product video (user-started, lazy) is the highest-value motion
  on a jewelry PDP; it shows sparkle that photos cannot.

### 20. Catbird, Mejuri (premium brands; design reference only)
- (a) Catbird: browse by price band, metal, stone, birth month; ring sizing and care guides;
  consultations, home try-on, in-store services; reviews linked to products.
- (b) Clear materials claims (solid gold vs plated), warranty, returns.
- (c) Mega-menu with promo tiles, best-sellers strip, value icons, editorial "world of" section.
- (d) Mejuri: auto-advancing hero carousel with a visible pause control on each slide
  (accessibility pattern worth following for any auto motion).
- Takeaway: shop-by-budget and occasion entry points, sizing guide, materials education.

### 21. Jewelers of America "Find a Jeweler"
- (a) Directory searchable by location, name, or online sellers; profile per member.
- (b) Members sign the **Code of Professional Practices every year**; "JA Certified" icon;
  "100 Club" for 100+ year old members.
- Takeaway: a seller code of conduct accepted at registration and re-confirmed yearly, shown on
  the store page, is a cheap, honest trust signal.

## Ideas ranked by Jeparla priority

1. Buyer trust: price breakup on PDP; hallmark (əyar damğası) photo; public verification
   methodology; seller code of conduct; measured store facts.
2. Seller acquisition: bulk product import from phone/spreadsheet; "for sellers" landing page
   with honest comparison to link-in-bio tools.
3. Conversion to WhatsApp: question templates in the WhatsApp message; open/closed state with
   expected reply time stated by the store; size guide to remove back-and-forth.
4. Distinctive restrained design/motion: user-started product video; second-image crossfade on
   cards; number transitions on the price widget; all transform/opacity, reduced-motion safe.
